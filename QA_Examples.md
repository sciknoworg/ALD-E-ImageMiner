
# 📊 Question–Answer Categories for Materials Science Figures

Following are **10 categories**, each containing **at least 5 Q\&A examples**, suitable for figures in **materials science literature**.

## 1. **Figure Identification & Description**

1. Q: What type of plot is shown in the figure?
   - A: It is a stress–strain curve.
2. Q: What do the x- and y-axes represent?
   - A: The x-axis is temperature (K), and the y-axis is thermal conductivity (W/m·K).
3. Q: What technique produced this spectrum?
   - A: It is an X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern.
4. Q: What is being visualized in the micrograph?
   - A: The grain morphology of a polycrystalline alloy.
5. Q: Which property is being measured in this bar chart
   - A: Vickers hardness of different heat-treated samples.

## 2. **Trend & Pattern Recognition**

1. Q: How does resistivity change as temperature increases?
   - A: Resistivity increases approximately linearly with temperature.
2. Q: What is the relationship between particle size and catalytic activity?
    - A: Catalytic activity increases as particle size decreases.
3. Q: How does fracture toughness vary with porosity?
   - A: Fracture toughness decreases with increasing porosity.
4. Q: What happens to the storage modulus as frequency increases?
    - A: The storage modulus increases with frequency.
5. Q: How does photocurrent vary with applied bias voltage?
    - A: Photocurrent increases sharply beyond 0.6 V.

## 3. **Quantitative Reading & Extraction**

1. Q: What is the bandgap energy estimated from the Tauc plot?
    - A: About 2.1 eV.
2. Q: What is the maximum stress sustained before fracture?
    - A: Approximately 950 MPa.
3. Q: At what temperature does the specific heat reach its peak?
    - A: Around 310 K.
4. Q: What is the saturation magnetization value?
    - A: About 60 emu/g.
5. Q: What is the ionic conductivity at 500 °C?
    - A: Approximately $5 \times 10^{-3} \, S/cm$.

## 4. **Comparative Reasoning**

1. Q: Which sample shows higher electrical conductivity, annealed or quenched?
    - A: The annealed sample shows higher conductivity.
2. Q: Between the two composites, which has higher flexural strength?
    - A: The fiber-reinforced composite.
3. Q: Which thin film absorbs more light in the UV region?
    - A: The doped thin film absorbs more strongly in the UV region.
4. Q: Which material demonstrates faster degradation in PBS solution?
    - A: The uncoated sample degrades faster.
5. Q: Which electrode retains higher capacity after 100 charge–discharge cycles?
    - A: The carbon-coated electrode.

## 5. **Cause–Effect Interpretation**

1. Q: Why does hardness increase after heat treatment?
    - A: Because of precipitation strengthening from secondary phases.
2. Q: Why does the curve show a plateau in the stress–strain plot?
   - A: Due to plastic deformation and dislocation motion.
3. Q: Why is there a sudden drop in transmittance at 400 nm?
   - A: Because the bandgap absorption edge occurs near that wavelength.
4. Q: Why does dielectric constant decrease with frequency?
  A: Because dipoles cannot reorient fast enough at high frequencies.
5. Q: Why is there a peak in magnetization around 350 K?
  A: It corresponds to the Curie temperature of the material.

## 6. **Uncertainty & Error Analysis**

1. Q: What do the shaded regions around the curves indicate?
    - A: They represent confidence intervals from repeated experiments.
2. Q: Which dataset has greater scatter in tensile strength values?
    - A: The powder-metallurgy sample set.
3. Q: Are the differences in thermal conductivity statistically significant?
    - A: No, because the error bars overlap across samples.
4. Q: Why might the DSC peak area vary between replicates?
    - A: Due to sample mass variations and instrumental sensitivity.
5. Q: What do the vertical lines on the XRD peaks represent?
    - A: Standard reference positions for the crystalline phase.

## 7. **Cross-Referencing with Theory/Models**

1. Q: Does the Hall–Petch relationship hold for this data?
    - A: Yes, strength increases with decreasing grain size.
2. Q: Is the resistivity vs. temperature data consistent with metallic behavior?
    - A: Yes, resistivity increases linearly with temperature.
3. Q: Does the conductivity follow an Arrhenius trend?
    - A: Yes, the log(σ) vs 1/T plot is linear.
4. Q: Is the stress relaxation behavior consistent with a viscoelastic model?
    - A: Yes, it follows an exponential decay characteristic of viscoelasticity.
5. Q: Does the magnetic susceptibility curve match Curie–Weiss law predictions?
    - A: Yes, it shows a linear dependence above the Curie temperature.

## 8. **Application & Implications**

1. Q: Based on the hardness data, is this alloy suitable for cutting tools?
    - A: Yes, its hardness is comparable to tool steels.
2. Q: Can this polymer membrane be used for fuel cells?
    - A: Yes, because its ionic conductivity exceeds 0.1 S/cm at 80 °C.
3. Q: Is the measured thermal expansion coefficient acceptable for aerospace applications?
    - A: Yes, it is low enough to maintain dimensional stability.
4. Q: Do the corrosion tests indicate suitability for marine environments?
    - A: Yes, the material shows low corrosion current densities.
5. Q: Does the optical absorption spectrum suggest potential for solar cells?
    - A: Yes, the material absorbs strongly in the visible range.

## 9. **Figure–Text Integration**

1. Q: How does the SEM micrograph support the claim of grain refinement?
    - A: It shows smaller, more uniform grains after processing.
2. Q: What evidence in the plot confirms the claim of improved toughness?
    - A: The area under the stress–strain curve is larger.
3. Q: How do the EDS spectra validate the presence of dopants?
    - A: They show characteristic peaks corresponding to the dopant element.
4. Q: What does the cyclic voltammetry curve indicate about catalytic activity?
    - A: It shows higher current density for the modified electrode.
5. Q: How do the impedance spectra support the claim of reduced charge transfer resistance?
    - A: The Nyquist plots show a smaller semicircle radius for the coated sample.

## 10. **Anomaly & Outlier Detection**

1. Q: Are there any anomalous peaks in the XRD pattern?
    - A: Yes, one peak at 42° does not match the reference phase.
2. Q: Which data point deviates from the stress–strain trend
    - A: The point at 0.15 strain and 700 MPa appears anomalous.
3. Q: Is there an unexpected feature in the DSC curve?
   - A: Yes, a small exothermic peak appears near 200 °C.
4. Q: Which sample shows an unexpected drop in conductivity?
    - A: The doped sample at 400 K.
5. Q: Are there outliers in the porosity vs. density scatter plot?
    - A: Yes, one data point shows unusually high porosity for its density.
